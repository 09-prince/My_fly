================================================================================
SWAT THE FLY
A real fruit fly's escape circuit, wired as measured, running in a browser
================================================================================

Last updated: 2026-09-25


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT THIS IS, IN ONE PARAGRAPH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

437 neurons taken straight out of the Drosophila connectome, wired to each
other exactly as they are in a real fly's brain, simulated as spiking cells,
and hooked up to a game. You send a bird at the fly; the fly dodges. The
decision to dodge -- whether, when, and which way -- is made by the neurons.
Nothing is trained. Every connection strength was measured from an actual
brain by electron microscopy, not learned or tuned.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE SHORT VERSION OF WHAT IS REAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

REAL (from the connectome, untouched):
  - which neuron connects to which
  - how many synapses each connection has (12,897 connections)
  - whether each neuron excites or inhibits
  - the left/right organisation of the circuit

INVENTED (my model, clearly the weak part):
  - the eye. The circuit starts several layers deep in the optic lobe, so
    everything in front of it is replaced by two numbers read off the screen.
  - the body. Once the Giant Fiber fires, how far and how fast the fly moves
    is arbitrary game physics.
  - the direction of the dodge. The firing is the fly's; mapping which Giant
    Fiber won onto "jump left / jump right" is my readout, not biology.
  - five simulation constants (see section 7).

NOT PRESENT AT ALL:
  - learning of any kind. There is no training, no dataset split, no loss.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE BIOLOGY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

When something rushes at a fly, this is the pathway that makes it jump:

    LC4 + LPLC2  ->  interneurons  ->  DNp01  ->  escape
    (looming             (124)        (the Giant
     detectors)                         Fiber)

LC4 and LPLC2 are "lobula columnar" cells. They sit deep in the optic lobe
and respond to looming -- an object expanding in the visual field. They are
not general motion detectors and they do not measure distance.

DNp01 is the Giant Fiber. It is a pair of descending neurons, one per side,
thick enough to see under a light microscope, running from the brain down to
the motor centres that drive the legs and wings. When DNp01 fires, the fly
takes off. It is one of the most-studied neurons in insect neuroscience.

This is the same decision a dinosaur in a jumping game has to make, which is
why the first version of this project drove the Chrome T-Rex game with it.

Honest note on predators: a crow will not hunt a fruit fly. Real predators
are jumping spiders, damselflies and frogs, and in the lab the stimulus is
usually a looming dark disc on a screen. The circuit does not care which --
it responds to anything growing fast in the visual field.

Honest note on escape direction: real flies set their escape direction with
leg posture roughly 200 ms BEFORE takeoff (Card & Dickinson, Current Biology
2008, "Visually mediated motor planning in the escape response of
Drosophila"). That postural planning is not in this circuit. The direction
shown here is derived from which Giant Fiber won, which is a modelling
choice, not a claim about flies.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Source: FlyEM male CNS v1.0, minimum synapse confidence 0.5.
Bucket: https://storage.googleapis.com/flyem-male-cns/v1.0/connectome-data/
        flat-connectome/

Three files, all public, no API token needed:

  connectome-weights-male-cns-v1.0-minconf-0.5.feather   1.0 GB
      151,856,684 rows: body_pre, body_post, weight
      Every synaptic connection in the male fly central nervous system.

  body-annotations-male-cns-v1.0-minconf-0.5.feather     14 MB
      211,577 rows: bodyId, instance, type, superclass, somaSide,
      somaLocation, status, and ~30 more columns.

  body-neurotransmitters-male-cns-v1.0.feather           43 MB
      1,835,518 rows. The column that matters is consensus_nt.

These files live in data/ and are gitignored. `python fly_dataset.py`
downloads whatever is missing.

                                     LC4 or LPLC2 (0 of 311 have them)

5. HOW THE CIRCUIT WAS EXTRACTED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

extract_circuit.py, roughly 3 minutes to run.

Inputs:   every Traced LC4 and LPLC2          -> 311 neurons
Output:   every Traced DNp01                  ->   2 neurons (10001, 10010)
Between:  a neuron is kept if it receives >= 5 synapses from the inputs AND
          sends >= 5 synapses to DNp01        -> 124 neurons

Every edge of weight >= 5 among those 437 neurons is kept: 12,897 edges.

The 152M-edge file is streamed batch by batch (2,318 Arrow record batches)
and filtered with pyarrow.compute.is_in. It is never loaded into pandas as a
whole -- that would need several GB of RAM.

Result: circuit.json, 196 KB. Also exported as web/circuit.js with a
`window.CIRCUIT =` prefix, because Chrome blocks fetch() from file:// and
the page has to work by double-clicking index.html.

Composition of the 437:
    LPLC2   185     LC4   126     interneurons  124     DNp01  2

Neurotransmitters (consensus_nt):
    acetylcholine   386   -> excitatory
    gaba             46   -> inhibitory
    glutamate         5   -> inhibitory
Sign convention: ACh +1, GABA and glutamate -1. This is the convention used
by whole-brain fly models. Glutamate is the shakiest of the three; in flies
it is usually inhibitory via GluCl, but not always.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. WHAT THE WIRING ACTUALLY SAYS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

These numbers were measured from the extracted circuit, not assumed. They
are the most interesting part of the project.

6a. DIRECT INPUT TO THE GIANT FIBERS IS STRICTLY ONE-EYED

    DNp01_L  <- left-eye detectors    6,418 synapses
             <- right-eye detectors       0
    DNp01_R  <- right-eye detectors   4,780 synapses
             <- left-eye detectors        0

    No direct left-to-right connection survives the 5-synapse cutoff. So
    which Giant Fiber fires already encodes which side the threat came from.

6b. THE INDIRECT PATHS CROSS, AND PULL THE OTHER WAY

    Routed through the interneurons (two-hop, signed weight products):

    left eye  -> interneurons -> DNp01_L    -1,355,898   (inhibits)
    left eye  -> interneurons -> DNp01_R      +444,086   (excites)
    right eye -> interneurons -> DNp01_R      -660,950   (inhibits)
    right eye -> interneurons -> DNp01_L      +935,948   (excites)

    So each eye inhibits its own Giant Fiber and excites the opposite one,
    through the interneurons. (The magnitudes are products of two weight
    matrices, so they are not directly comparable to the one-hop numbers --
    different units. Only the signs and the relative pattern matter.)

    This has a very concrete consequence, seen repeatedly during
    development: turn the detector gain up and the fly reacts sooner but
    dodges toward the WRONG SIDE, because the crossed path reaches
    threshold before the direct one does. Section 7 explains how the gain
    was chosen because of this.

6c. LC4 IS WEIGHTED ROUGHLY TWICE AS HEAVILY AS LPLC2

    per-neuron signed weight onto DNp01:
        LC4     25.25
        LPLC2   13.07
        interneurons  -1.54  (net inhibitory)

    LC4 carries the growth-rate signal and LPLC2 the size signal. The
    circuit weights growth rate about twice as heavily. This is not
    something the model was built to produce -- and it predicted a result
    (section 8a) before that result was measured.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. THE MODEL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

7a. THE EYE (invented -- web/vision.js)

The circuit begins at LC4 and LPLC2, which are already several synapses deep
in the optic lobe. Everything before them -- photoreceptors, lamina, medulla
-- is not simulated. In its place:

    theta     = 2 * atan(r / distance)      how big the object looks
    dtheta/dt = rate of change of theta     how fast it is growing

Azimuth decides which eye sees it. Fly eyes face sideways with a narrow
frontal overlap, modelled as +/- 0.52 rad (about 30 degrees) either side of
straight ahead; inside that band the object is shared between the eyes,
outside it goes wholly to one.

    theta      -> recruits LPLC2   (how big)
    dtheta/dt  -> recruits LC4     (how fast)

"Recruits" means: the stronger the signal, the more of that type fire, taken
in a fixed seeded order. LC4 and LPLC2 have no retinotopic coordinates in
this dataset, so there is no principled way to give each cell a patch of the
visual field. Recruitment is the honest fallback -- and it looks right,
since more dots light up as the threat closes.

7b. THE NEURONS (real wiring -- web/fly.js)

Leaky integrate-and-fire, one unit per neuron, one step per frame:

    W[i][j]  = synapse_count * sign(i) * w_scale      (* inhibition if sign<0)

    v  = v * tau + drive + (spikes . W)
    spike where v >= threshold, then v = 0
    v floored at -2

    rate_L = rate_L * rate_tau + spike(DNp01_L) * (1 - rate_tau)
    rate_R likewise

    escape when max(rate_L, rate_R) >= jump_rate
    direction from which of the two is higher

The two Giant Fibers are read SEPARATELY. An earlier version averaged them,
which capped the readout at 0.5 when only one side fired and made one-sided
dodges impossible. That was a real bug.

7c. THE CONSTANTS (web/tuning.js)

    theta_gain     0.55    theta      -> fraction of LPLC2 recruited
    dtheta_gain    6       dtheta/dt  -> fraction of LC4 recruited
    drive          1.6     current delivered to a recruited neuron
    tau            0.72    membrane leak per step
    threshold      1.0     spike threshold
    w_scale        0.0005  synapse count -> current
    inhibition     2.6     extra strength for GABA/glutamate
    jump_rate      0.08    DNp01 firing rate that triggers escape
    rate_tau       0.55    smoothing on that rate
    smooth         0.6     smoothing on dtheta/dt (columns are discrete)
    escape_speed   28      takeoff speed in px/step  (arena only, not biology)

w_scale is the one constant that was CALIBRATED rather than searched: it was
derived from the measured per-neuron weight onto DNp01 so that threshold is
crossed at roughly 25% recruitment. The first guess was 0.020, which is 28x
too high -- the whole network saturated and the Giant Fiber fired constantly
from the moment anything entered view. Working it out from the actual
synapse counts is what made the model work at all.

The other three (theta_gain, dtheta_gain, jump_rate) come from a grid search
with four HARD constraints, all of which must hold:

    1. never hit, at any throw speed or angle
    2. the Giant Fiber matching the side the threat came from must be the
       one that fires
    3. no flinch when something passes wide
    4. no firing at all when nothing is there

Constraint 2 is what keeps the gain low. Higher gains give longer warning
(98 px instead of 70 px) but flip the side, for the reason in section 6b.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. RESULTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

8a. THE T-REX VERSION (the first build, still published)

Driving the Chrome dinosaur game. Mean steps survived over 20 obstacle
sequences the parameters were never fitted on. A run caps at 3000 steps, so
3000 means it never crashed.

    fly brain, theta + dtheta/dt      3000    20/20 solved
    fly brain, dtheta/dt only         3000    20/20 solved
    fly brain, theta only              373     0/20
    random jumping                     159     0/20

    Angular SIZE alone fails. GROWTH RATE alone is enough.

That asymmetry was not designed in. It falls out of the wiring, where LC4 --
the growth-rate channel -- carries roughly twice the synaptic weight onto
DNp01 per neuron that LPLC2 does (section 6c). The measurement predicted the
behaviour.

8b. THE ARENA VERSION (current)

    survived direct attacks     63/63    (7 speeds x 9 angles)
    correct Giant Fiber fired    8/8
    flinch at a near miss        0/15
    false alarms while idle      0 in 900 steps
    escape lead              70-140 px before contact

    Several birds at once:
        two from the same side      0/7 hit,  0/7 wrong side
        five at once                0/7 hit
        eight at once               0/7 hit
        three converging, max speed 1/7 hit   <- the only way to win

    Being hit by three birds converging from three sides at maximum speed is
    not a detection failure: the Giant Fiber still fires in time. The fly
    simply has nowhere left to land. That was left in rather than tuned away.

8c. DRIFT (a bug found by the user, now guarded)

Every escape moved the fly and nothing brought it back, so a sustained
barrage walked it across the arena until it was pinned against an edge with
no room to dodge. Measured over a 2400-step barrage, then letting it settle:

    before   fly ends 782-933 px from its starting spot
    after    fly ends     0-1 px from its starting spot
    hits: 6 either way -- the fix makes it neither easier nor harder

It now eases back at 0.045 per step. Faster returns (0.09, 0.22) nearly
TRIPLED the hits, because the fly hurries back into birds still incoming.

9. THE APP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Open web/index.html in a browser. No server, no build step, no dependencies.

    drag          send a bird -- several at once is allowed
    space         pause
    R             replay the last attack at quarter speed
    Reset         clear the counts and put the fly back
    slider        throw speed, 2 to 14 px/step

Three panels, all on one screen:

  THE GRASS      Top-down. The fly faces LEFT, so its left eye covers the
                 lower half of the arena and its right eye the upper half.
                 The two faint wedges are its visual fields; they brighten
                 with how much that eye is seeing. The grass, soil and
                 clover are scenery -- the fly is never shown them, only the
                 birds.

  GIANT FIBER    Vertical, on the right. Time runs top to bottom, firing
                 rate across. The shaded band is everything past threshold;
                 a line bulging into it IS the decision to jump.

  CONNECTOME     All 437 neurons at their real soma positions, seen from
                 the front, at true proportions -- the bilateral shape is
                 the fly's own: two optic lobes flanking the central brain,
                 with the pair of Giant Fibers between them. LPLC2 sits
                 dorsolateral, LC4 below it, interneurons through the
                 middle.

                 All 12,897 connections are drawn. Dim ones are baked into
                 a backdrop once; the ones carrying a spike this step are
                 drawn live on top. A bird from the right sets the right
                 optic lobe ablaze and leaves the left half dark -- that is
                 the lateralisation of section 6a, visible as wiring inside
                 a real head.

                 x is MIRRORED, because left-side somata sit at higher x in
                 the volume. Without the flip a threat on the fly's left
                 would light the right of the picture.

                 Either side: how many of each detector bank are firing,
                 and that side's Giant Fiber.

                 The soft glow is one blurred blob per soma. It is the real
                 soma distribution, not an anatomical outline -- there is no
                 brain mesh in this build.

Replay re-runs the simulation from the same starting state rather than
playing back recorded frames, so slow motion shows the real thing. It does
not add to the escape/hit counts.

12. THINGS THAT WENT WRONG, AND WHAT THEY TAUGHT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Kept because each one is a trap worth remembering.

  fetch_adjacencies returns (neurons, connections), not the other way round.
  The original scripts unpacked it backwards and saved neuron lists into
  files named *_edges.csv. They contained no edges at all.

  w_scale guessed at 0.020 was 28x too high. Guessing a scale for real
  synapse counts does not work; derive it from the counts.

  Averaging the two Giant Fibers capped the readout at 0.5 and made
  one-sided dodges impossible.

  The T-Rex physics was unwinnable at first: the dino cleared each obstacle
  and then landed on its trailing edge, dying at step ~102 on every seed.
  Airtime covered 7.15 units where 8.6 were needed.

  Left-side somata sit at HIGHER x in the volume. A brain view that does not
  mirror puts a left-eye response on the right of the screen.

  Higher gain gives more warning and the wrong answer (section 6b). Any
  change that raises the total drive -- bigger objects, more objects, higher
  gain -- can flip the side. Always re-run the side check.

  Plotting soma positions produces a cloud of dots that says nothing. Soma
  position is where the cell body sits, not what it is wired to.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
13. WHAT WOULD MAKE THIS BETTER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - A real optic lobe. Include the medulla and lobula cells upstream of LC4
    and LPLC2 and feed actual pixels in. This removes the invented front end,
    which is the largest weakness by far.
  - Reservoir readout: freeze the circuit, train one small classifier on the
    descending-neuron activity. That would be an honest ML contribution --
    "can this circuit linearly encode time-to-contact?"
  - Evolution strategies on the readout instead of a grid search on five
    constants.
  - Check the extracted subgraph against the published escape-circuit
    literature rather than trusting a 5-synapse cutoff.
  - The glutamate sign assumption (5 neurons) deserves checking.
