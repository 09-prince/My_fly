What decides the dodge

LC4 and LPLC2 are looming detectors sitting deep in the fly's optic lobe. When something rushes at the fly they drive DNp01 — the Giant Fiber, a pair of neurons so thick you can see them under a light microscope. DNp01 firing is what launches the escape.

A crow will not actually hunt a fruit fly. What eats them is jumping spiders, damselflies and frogs, and in the lab it is usually a looming dark disc on a screen. The circuit does not care which: it responds to anything that grows fast in the visual field, which is what all of them have in common.

The direct wiring gives each Giant Fiber one eye. DNp01_L receives 6,418 synapses from left-eye detectors and none from the right; DNp01_R receives 4,780 from the right and none from the left. No direct left-to-right connection survives the five-synapse cutoff used here, so which Giant Fiber fires says which side the threat came from.

The indirect wiring does cross, and it pulls the other way. Routed through the interneurons, right-eye input excites the left Giant Fiber while inhibiting the right one, and the mirror holds for the left eye. Turn the detector gain up and this crossed path wins: the fly reacts sooner but dodges toward the wrong side. The gain used here is the highest that still lets the direct input decide.

Try a near miss

Aim wide and the fly does not flinch. Nothing in the circuit measures distance — it responds to growth rate, and a bird passing to one side never grows fast enough to reach threshold. That is the looming model making a prediction you can falsify by hand.

Sending several at once is the only reliable way to win. Each eye responds to the strongest looming signal in view, so extra birds do not simply add up — but they do raise the total drive, and they take away the space the fly needs to land in. Surround it from three sides at full speed and it still fires in time, with nowhere left to go.

A slow throw still works, just later. The fly waits until the growth rate crosses threshold, which at low speed happens closer in.

Honest limits

The wiring, the synapse counts and the excitatory or inhibitory signs are real. The eyes are not: everything in front of LC4 and LPLC2 is replaced by the angular size each eye would see, which is the invented part of this model.

The direction of the dodge is this model's readout of which Giant Fiber won, not a claim about the fly. Real flies set escape direction with leg posture roughly 200 ms before takeoff (Card & Dickinson, 2008), which is not in this circuit.
